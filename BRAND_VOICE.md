# MarginSignal OS — Brand Voice

We are the independent referee for whether deployed AI actually worked. Every page — and every future post, deck, or email — should sound like *that* company: confident, plain, and impossible to mistake for a vendor grading its own scorecard.

## The seven rules
1. **Lead with the uncomfortable question, not the product.** ("Did your AI actually work?") Outcome first, feature last.
2. **Plain and blunt. Short, then long.** Use contrast pairs — *"Monitoring proves it ran. It can't prove it worked."* Cut throat-clearing and jargon stacks.
3. **Independence is the hook.** Close the thought on it: *"…because it isn't yours." "Verifiable by a party that doesn't trust the operator."*
4. **One idea per line. End on the hook.**
5. **AI leads; CPR is the instrument, not the headline.** CPR is *how* we measure the outcome — never the lead.
6. **Confident, never salesy.** Capability-level only. No fake clients, no invented results, never "patented" (it's **patent-pending**). Credibility is the product.
7. **Provocative on the idea, never on people.** Earn mentions by being cited, not by pile-ons — controversy corrodes the neutrality moat.

## Words we use
independent · verify / verification · neutral · outcome · "did it work" · fault attribution · agent / agentic · anchored · reproducible · patent-pending · resolution

## Words we retire (old contact-center voice)
"margin diagnostic" · "forensic margin" · "on the floor" · "Exposure Review" as a lead · "Signal Scan" as the headline · leading with CPR or fault-lines *before* the AI outcome

## The test
Read any headline aloud. If it could sit on a contact-center cost-cutting consultancy's site, it's wrong. If it sounds like the independent body that grades whether the AI worked, it's right.
